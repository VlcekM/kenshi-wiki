The **World States** system allows for the player's actions to have lasting consequences on the surrounding world. This affects mainly [Town Overrides](/town-overrides) and the spawn rate of [Homeless Spawns](/heng-zone#homeless-spawns) in [Zones](/category/zones).

## Introduction

The players can influence the world of [Kenshi](/kenshi) in a number of ways. The most prominent (and straightforward) is the killing or kidnapping of the [important leader(s) of factions](/category/bosses). For a smaller faction (such as the [Skin Bandits](/skin-bandits)), killing their leader can lead to a vast reduction in the spawn rate for squads of that faction. The faction base is then destroyed or occupied by a different faction. Several of the factions may themselves also have questlines that may be part of one or many [Routes](/world-state-routes) which require certain achievements of World States.

Generally speaking, it doesn't matter whether you Imprison or Kill a faction leader, the results will remain the same except in a few cases<sup id="cite_ref-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-1"><span class="cite-bracket">[</span>1<span class="cite-bracket">]</span></a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-2"><span class="cite-bracket">[</span>2<span class="cite-bracket">]</span></a></sup>. When certain condition states that it requires a person to be dead, imprisoning said character will also work. Conversely, if a condition states that it requires a person to be alive, it means that they should be alive AND free (not imprisoned). If (in [FCS](/forgotten-construction-set)) a condition checks whether or not a person is "Okay", it means whether or not they are both alive AND free.

Killing the leader of a larger faction will not necessarily lead to the crippling of that faction. Instead, the player will need to find the other faction leaders and deal with them. These larger factions, such as [The Holy Nation](/the-holy-nation) and the [United Cities](/united-cities), will likely send increasingly strong [Base Assaults](/base-assault) if the player chooses to kill a minor faction leader before killing the primary leader, up until the point of the largest assaults, which would usually persist indefinitely and be launched every few days or weeks.

With a few exceptions<sup id="cite_ref-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-3"><span class="cite-bracket">[</span>3<span class="cite-bracket">]</span></a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-4"><span class="cite-bracket">[</span>4<span class="cite-bracket">]</span></a></sup>, it is impossible to completely destroy a faction (and their military) entirely as their squads would usually just be replaced with a smaller 'broken' squad<sup id="cite_ref-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-5"><span class="cite-bracket">[</span>5<span class="cite-bracket">]</span></a></sup>, and in some cases still retain a notable number of settlements<sup id="cite_ref-6" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-6"><span class="cite-bracket">[</span>6<span class="cite-bracket">]</span></a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-7" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-7"><span class="cite-bracket">[</span>7<span class="cite-bracket">]</span></a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-8" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-8"><span class="cite-bracket">[</span>8<span class="cite-bracket">]</span></a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-9" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-9"><span class="cite-bracket">[</span>9<span class="cite-bracket">]</span></a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-10" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-10"><span class="cite-bracket">[</span>10<span class="cite-bracket">]</span></a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-11" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-11"><span class="cite-bracket">[</span>11<span class="cite-bracket">]</span></a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-12" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-12"><span class="cite-bracket">[</span>12<span class="cite-bracket">]</span></a></sup>. In the cases of [Base Assaults](/base-assault), it's possible for it to be permanent<sup id="cite_ref-13" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-13"><span class="cite-bracket">[</span>13<span class="cite-bracket">]</span></a></sup>.

If a character tied with homeless spawns was kidnapped or imprisoned but was later released, the homeless spawns tied with said character will all come back to effect. This appears to be intended as it likely accounts for successful rescue operations intended to bring their leader back alive<sup id="cite_ref-14" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-14"><span class="cite-bracket">[</span>14<span class="cite-bracket">]</span></a></sup>, [Town Overrides](/town-overrides) may also switch in certain cases<sup id="cite_ref-15" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-15"><span class="cite-bracket">[</span>15<span class="cite-bracket">]</span></a></sup>, in some cases it is even _necessary_ for the person needed to be initially killed/imprisoned to achieve the override to be _later released_ for the override to persist<sup id="cite_ref-16" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-16"><span class="cite-bracket">[</span>16<span class="cite-bracket">]</span></a></sup>.

If a player somehow manages to recruit a character tied to world states (such as [Seto](/seto) or [Bo](/bo)), the relevant world state effects will still apply.

## Trivia

-   Despite popular misconception, many of the overrides that are labelled "Prosperous" in the [FCS](/forgotten-construction-set) does not necessarily mean that the town/city/village in question is more prosperous than it was before and may even be at a worse condition (e.g. more buildings destroyed or more malnourished individuals). Rather, it is considered a "Prosperous" override because when compared to all the other overrides for said area, the override in question is the best one it could achieve.
