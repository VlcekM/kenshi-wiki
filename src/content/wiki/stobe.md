**Stobe** is a character in Kenshi lore. The two zones [Stobe's Gamble](/stobes-gamble) and [Stobe's Garden](/stobes-garden) are named in honor of him. His remains can be found in Stobe's Garden, east of a [Reaver Camp](/reaver-camp) and west of [Ark](/ark). 

The [Skeleton Bandits](/skeleton-bandits) often speak about Stobe, and acknowledge him as a deity.<sup id="cite_ref-Skeleton_Bandits_1-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Skeleton_Bandits-1"><span class="cite-bracket">[</span>1<span class="cite-bracket">]</span></a></sup> Many Skeletons<sup id="cite_ref-Stobe's_Garden_2-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Stobe's_Garden-2"><span class="cite-bracket">[</span>2<span class="cite-bracket">]</span></a></sup> are apparently saddened by the sight of Stobe's carcass (or at least being in proximity of him). Although it is unclear what exactly he has done, if anything.

A few speculations could be made.

-   He _might_ be a behemoth. Stobe, while being significantly larger than your average Skeleton, is also significantly smaller than the Behemoth bodies found in Obedience, whilst also looking significantly different. Most Behemoths seemingly died in the massacre at [Obedience](/obedience) or in the war leading up to it.
    -   It could be possible that Stobe was a Second Empire imitation of First Empire Behemoths, especially considering that they were hinted to be a desperate imitation of their predecessors<sup id="cite_ref-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-3"><span class="cite-bracket">[</span>3<span class="cite-bracket">]</span></a></sup>, albeit much smaller in size, and using a [Screamer Unit](/screamer-unit) Template similar to other Second Empire soldiers.
-   Skeletons do not seem to agree on what Stobe was supposed to represent and achieve. [Elder](/elder), the leader of [Skeleton Bandits](/skeleton-bandits), calls him an "exterminator of the human plague", but also a "martyr".<sup id="cite_ref-Elder_4-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Elder-4"><span class="cite-bracket">[</span>4<span class="cite-bracket">]</span></a></sup>
    -   If a Skeleton character interacts with Elder, the only response that the said Skeleton can state in response to Elder's dialogue is that Stobe being "exterminator of the human plague" is a lie and that Elder himself is also aware of it.<sup id="cite_ref-Elder_4-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Elder-4"><span class="cite-bracket">[</span>4<span class="cite-bracket">]</span></a></sup> This is verifiable as true given the existence of Humans in Kenshi (the "human plague" here seems to be referring to "humans as _the_ plague" rather than "plague that kills humans"). However, it's unclear if Stobe had the intention of exterminating humans but failed, or did not have such intention to begin with.
    -   In the same branch of dialogue above, Elder also states that calling Stobe "exterminator of the human plague" is a "justice" that is "ironic". And that through this "Stobe is finally getting the recognition he deserves" and "a fitting retribution to top it off". It is unclear or at least not explicitly stated in what exact sense it's ironical in, who the target of retribution is, and if Elder wants Stobe to have recognition out of veneration or vilification for/against him.

Stobe's death is mostly shrouded in mystery. Stobe's 'Gamble', as a name, seems to imply it was the site where he was either fighting or taking a gambit at, with the 'Garden' being where he passed away or was laid to rest.

Not too far from where Stobe lay dead, you can find what appears to be a rocket or missile partially buried in the sand. Unlike most random space junk in the world, this world item seems deliberately placed. Perhaps this was the Ancients' contingency plan to end life on Kenshi following their collapse or abandonment of the island/planet, which Stobe sacrificed himself to stop. This would make more sense than a showdown where Stobe was 'killed' by an army or another Behemoth, as whatever killed him would then go on to kill the other races he tried to preserve.

In the case of a rocket or missile, it would not go on to kill—even if Stobe dies in the process of stopping it.

There are clearly holes in this theory, the most prominent of which include:

1.  Missiles will still detonate on impact. If Stobe crashed himself towards the rocket, it should still explode mid-air (assuming Stobe could somehow fly) or on-ground and resulting explosion would still kill everyone in proximity.
2.  Stobe and the remains of the Missile are located at a significant distance from each other, almost an entire mountain away. If Stobe 'absorbed' the missile, it should be either on top, inside or beside his carcass.
3.  The missile/rocket appears to be mostly retaining it's original shape. If said missile/rocket exploded and killed Stobe in the process, the resulting explosion should disintegrate and fragment the entire missile/rocket and cause metal scraps to fly at all directions. The result of which should make said missile completely unrecognizable.

But it seems the most plausible given the lack of additional information in-game and environmental storytelling surrounding Stobe and the area his husk inhabits.

## Speculations on connections with Chitrin

According to the [Book of Sacrifice](/book-of-sacrifice), it told the story of Father Chitrin, an ancient deity which at one point saved the planet from some unknown calamity. Judging by the theory already presented, Stobe's status and reverence as a "martyr" and the inclusion of the giant wrecked missile points to several possible conclusions.

1.  **Chitrin and Stobe are the Same person and is a Behemoth**
    1.  According to the [Book of Sacrifice](/book-of-sacrifice), Chitrin was "betrayed" by his "children" but yet despite this betrayal, he appeared forgiving of their "sins" and thus sacrificed himself to save them from calamity. The almost mythical status of a savior and martyr is shared between the two, alluding to them being one of the same.
    2.  Secondly, according to the dialogue spoken when entering [Obedience](/obedience) with a Skeleton, Stobe's unwavering devotion to save his children despite their "sins" and "betrayals" shares the same flaws as those of the Behemoths because of their own sense of "blind obedience and unquestioning loyalty" to their human masters.
2.  **The Skeletons revolted against Humanity and launched a dooms-day weapon**
    1.  The Obedience dialogue made it clear that "man became afraid of the destructive capabilities of its own creations" and thus the First Empire most likely consisted of Human masters as the First Empire's destruction were repeatedly mentioned to have been biological in nature. This would explain how the Skeletons were able to survive the First Extinction.
    2.  Because of what happened within Obedience and the Behemoths, it can be assumed that the Skeletons feared for their own destruction and thus revolted against their Human masters.
    3.  The [Okanite theories books](/okranite-theories-in-relation-to-findings-series) as well as the Mass Extinctions book all follow a similar theme of conflict between the Humans and Skeletons at some point in the past. This may explain why Okranites abhor Skeletons in their religious doctrine, because of the sentiment that they nearly wiped out humanity from the planet.
    4.  The book "[Scripture of Radiance Vol I.](/scripture-of-radiance-series)" described the myth of a great battle which took place within the skies above between Narko and her Skeleton minions against Okran and his human followers supporting the theory of the missile-strike being the main event which almost spelt the doom of Humanity.
3.  **Stobe sacrified himself to save Humanity from the First Extinction.**
    1.  According to the book [Mass Extinction III](/mass-extinction-series), it has been hypothesized by Atticus that a doomsday weapon of a biological nature was used to wipe out the First Empire, with a further theory stating that "an attack could have easily been carried out by the skeletons themselves."
    2.  This is further supported by the wreckage of a massive missile next to where Stobe died.
    3.  The book "[Okranite Theories In Relation To Findings Series I](/okranite-theories-in-relation-to-findings-series)" also supports the theory that a small group of surviving humans was saved from total extinction by the hands of a great savior.
4.  **The Skeletons felt immense guilt for what they've done and thus sought to recreate Civilization.**
    1.  The sacrifice of Stobe on behalf of the humans was likely the reason why many Skeletons felt immense amount of guilt for their genocide against humans as evident with the [CPU of Cat-Lon](/cpu-of-cat-lon), which mentioned that "Cat-Lon's dream was to rebuild civilisation and make up for past mistakes" and to "undo the crimes and errors of Skeleton-kind and redeem themselves to mankind"
    2.  This sense of guilt is best expressed within the book "[The Mystery of Skeletech II](/the-mystery-of-skeletech-series)", describing the sudden mass suicides of skeletons at an unknown point in history.
    3.  This may also be a reason why many Skeletons suffer from memory loss and amnesia, so as to forget the misdeeds of their past. Others, like [Iyo](/iyo) in the University of Machinists, actively try to hide the Skeletons involvement with the First Extinction based on an interaction if the player is also a Skeleton "Look, you don't need to worry... no one will ever know the truth. As long as I am Finch's second-in-command, I will see to that myself."
    4.  Thus, the [Second Empire](/second-empire) was most likely formed as a way to recreate civilization and to shepherd and protect the surviving humans as atonement for the Skeletons' sins. It is from the Second Empire that the Okranites first began to worship Stobe/Chitrin and thus became the progenitor of the [Holy Nation](/the-holy-nation).
    5.  This is further supported by the [CPU of Hat-12](/cpu-of-general-hat-12) which states that he " launched a crackdown on the new cultists but his heavy handed approach on fueled the blame and confusion further. Things escalated faster". This brutal crackdown likely reignited the Okranites hatred of machines and was also the catalyst which led to the origins of the Holy Nation.

## Trivia

Stobe's sacrifice and his involvement in stopping the missile strike which would've destroyed the last humans shares similarities with the movie [Iron Giant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Iron_Giant).
